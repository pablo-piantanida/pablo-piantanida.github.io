        ### From information theory to the mathematical foundations of reliable and collaborative intelligence

        **How can intelligent systems determine what information to acquire, what computations to perform, what evidence to trust, and when to communicate or act?**

        My research develops mathematical and algorithmic foundations for understanding how intelligent systems learn, reason, evaluate evidence, and make decisions under uncertainty and limited resources.

        Building on information theory, statistics, and statistical decision theory, I investigate the fundamental limits of **decision-relevant information processing**: what information must be represented or acquired to solve a task, when additional computation or interaction provides genuine value, and what guarantees can be established from finite evidence.

        This perspective connects three complementary research directions: **the mathematical foundations of learning and reasoning; uncertainty, evaluation, and reliable decision-making; and collaborative, resource-aware intelligence.**

        My long-term objective is to develop a unified theoretical framework in which learning, reasoning, information acquisition, communication, and action are understood as interconnected operations, guided by their contribution to reliable decisions.
