## How can we know when an AI system needs help?

AI systems increasingly tackle tasks that involve planning, retrieving information, writing code, using tools, and collaborating with other agents. A correct final answer tells us only part of the story. Did the system respect the user’s constraints? Did it use appropriate evidence? Did it stay within its resource budget? Could an intervention have prevented a failure?

This PhD will develop methods to **evaluate AI systems as they work and determine when supervision improves their decisions**. The research will combine mathematical modelling with experiments on language-model agents, connecting AI evaluation, uncertainty, and sequential decision-making.

## The research opportunity

A central direction is to study an external supervisor that observes an AI agent’s execution and decides whether to intervene. The supervisor could remind the agent of a forgotten constraint, challenge an unsupported assumption, or request a check before the task proceeds. It would also provide the user with an account of what happened and the evidence supporting its assessment.

This raises a fundamental question. **When does an intervention provide enough benefit to justify its cost and its potential to disrupt a successful execution?**

The supervisor sees only part of the system’s state and usually has no access to the correct answer while the task is running. It must therefore reason from incomplete evidence. The PhD will investigate how to make those decisions and how to evaluate their consequences.

## Questions you could explore

- **Evaluation beyond the final answer.** How should we assess task success, adherence to user constraints, resource use, and the need for human intervention?
- **Predicting failures during execution.** What can an agent’s messages, tool calls, and intermediate results reveal about its likelihood of succeeding?
- **Deciding when to intervene.** When should a supervisor remain silent, ask for verification, redirect the agent, or seek human input?
- **Supervision with limited resources.** Can a smaller or less expensive model effectively supervise a more capable agent?
- **Evidence for the user.** How can execution reports help people assess whether the system followed their instructions and whether further checking is needed?

The precise emphasis will evolve with the doctoral researcher’s interests and the findings of the project.

## Approach and expected contributions

The work will draw on statistical decision theory, information theory, uncertainty estimation, and modern machine learning. It will involve both formal analysis and the implementation of evaluation and supervision methods.

Experiments could examine language and reasoning tasks, information retrieval, and tool-using agents. The objective is to understand when supervision helps, when it introduces additional errors, and how its value depends on the task, the available evidence, and the user’s priorities.

Potential contributions include evaluation protocols, methods for predicting failures, intervention policies, and reproducible experimental tools.

We welcome applicants with a strong background in computer science, mathematics, statistics, engineering, or a related discipline, and an interest in understanding how AI systems behave.

Depending on the project’s emphasis, relevant interests include probability, statistical inference, natural language processing, sequential decision-making, and experimental evaluation. Candidates may bring complementary theoretical or practical strengths.
